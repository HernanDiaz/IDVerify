\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

All experiments are conducted on a single workstation running Windows~11,
equipped with an AMD Ryzen~5 3400G (3.70\,GHz), 32\,GB DDR4-3200 RAM, and an NVIDIA
RTX~5060\,Ti (16\,GB VRAM), using PyTorch 2.10.0 with CUDA 13.0 and
Optuna~3.x. Training uses a batch size of 64 and no data augmentation:
all images are resized to $224\!\times\!224$ pixels and normalised to
$[0,1]$ prior to caching in GPU memory.
\rev{This choice is deliberate: applying standard augmentation (rotation
$\pm10^\circ$, brightness, contrast, Gaussian blur, and JPEG compression)
significantly degraded every metric on the FantasyID holdout
(Dice $0.875\!\to\!0.780$, PR-AUC $0.997\!\to\!0.958$; all $p<10^{-7}$, paired
Wilcoxon over $n=30$ seeds), as the clean synthetic test distribution penalises
augmentation-induced train--test mismatch; per-seed results are archived
externally~\citep{docverify_zenodo}.}
The full NCV pipeline required
approximately 59~hours; VRAM caching reduces per-trial training time from
$\sim$12 to $\sim$3.5 minutes.

\subsection{Final Model Configuration}
\label{subsec:final_config}

\rev{The cross-validation above yields ten fold-specific configurations and
estimates generalization, but the deployed system is a single model. We select
the configuration with the smallest inner-CV distance to the ideal point
($1,1$) across all outer folds (Eq.~\ref{eq:ideal_point};
Table~\ref{tab:final_config}), which fixes both
the architecture (decoder width) and the Pareto-optimized loss weight
$\lambda_{\text{mask}}=2.46$, consistent with the $2.3$--$2.7$ cluster of
Section~\ref{subsec:ncv_results}. This final configuration is retrained from
scratch on the development set (up to 100 epochs with early stopping, as in
Section~\ref{subsec:ncv}) and evaluated on the held-out 15\% blind-test
set; we repeat the retraining over 30 independent seeds to quantify
run-to-run variability. All comparisons in the remainder of this section share
this 30-seed blind-test protocol.}

To isolate each design decision, we evaluate this final model against five
variants under the same 30-seed blind-test protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Three variants probe the architecture and loss
weighting: \texttt{cls\_only} (classification head only); \texttt{seg\_only}
(segmentation head only, detection by thresholding mean mask probability);
and \texttt{unweighted\_losses} (full multitask, $\lambda_{\text{cls}} =
\lambda_{\text{mask}} = 0.5$, bypassing HPO). \rev{Two further variants probe
the HPO selection criterion applied to the \emph{same} 500 MOTPE trials:
\texttt{by\_prauc} (the trial maximizing validation PR-AUC) and
\texttt{by\_dice} (the trial maximizing validation Dice), against our Pareto
criterion (distance to the ideal point).}

Regarding the architecture and loss variants, three findings stand out.
First, joint multitask training is essential:
\texttt{cls\_only} produces near-zero Dice ($0.002 \pm 0.008$). Second, a
dedicated classification head is necessary: \texttt{seg\_only} achieves
competitive Dice ($0.867$) but PR-AUC drops to $0.728 \pm 0.053$, confirming
that the segmentation logit alone is an unreliable detection signal. Third,
Pareto-optimized weights outperform equal weights specifically on localization:
\texttt{unweighted\_losses} matches the full model on PR-AUC ($p = 0.083$) but
shows significantly lower Dice ($p = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}$, $d = 1.01$, large
effect), validating the HPO contribution.

A key question for any multi-objective HPO framework is whether the added
complexity over simpler scalar alternatives yields measurable gains. \rev{Pareto
significantly outperforms both scalar criteria after Holm correction:
it improves localization over \texttt{by\_prauc} (Dice $+0.016$, $d=0.59$) and
improves \emph{both} objectives over \texttt{by\_dice} (PR-AUC $+0.0019$,
$d=0.64$; Dice $+0.018$, $d=1.00$, large effect).}

Beyond predictive accuracy, Pareto selection requires no a priori commitment to a
primary objective, making the full trade-off landscape observable
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}) and remaining structurally consistent with the DeepID
challenge protocol, which ranks detection and localization independently.

Table~\ref{tab:challenge} compares DocVerify against the top participants of
the DeepID 2025 Challenge on Track~1 (detection F1@0.5) and Track~2
(per-image localization F1), computed on our 15\% holdout using 30 seeds.

This comparison must be interpreted with caution. DocVerify was developed
independently after the DeepID 2025 Challenge submission deadline had already
closed; official test-set evaluation was therefore not possible and results
are reported on our internal 15\% holdout, whereas all challenge figures
were obtained on the official test set (FantasyID test partition plus a
private out-of-domain set from PXL Vision). The two evaluation sets are
not identical, making direct numerical comparisons indicative rather than
strictly controlled.

It is also important to note the training data disparity among the systems
in Table~\ref{tab:challenge}. Sunlight augmented the FantasyID training set
with over 60\,000 external identity document images; the TruFor baseline
was pre-trained on 876\,000 general-purpose manipulation images before any
fine-tuning. \rev{The third-place entry corresponds to the EdgeDoc+TruFor
\emph{fusion} submission (F1@0.5 $= 0.958$~\citep{deepid2025challenge}), which also
relies on the pre-trained TruFor backbone; the EdgeDoc architecture trained
on FantasyID alone reaches F1@0.5 $= 0.43$ (\citealp{edgedoc2025}, Table~2).
By contrast, EdgeDoc (standalone), UAM-Biometrics, and DocVerify train
exclusively on FantasyID, making them more directly comparable to one another.}

With that caveat, DocVerify achieves F1@0.5 $= 0.969 \pm 0.014$ on
Track~1. \rev{Because this is measured on a different evaluation set, we do not
claim superiority over any challenge entry; we report it only to situate
DocVerify among the FantasyID-only methods (EdgeDoc standalone $0.43$,
UAM-Biometrics $0.712$) and the externally pre-trained systems (fusion $0.958$,
Sunlight $0.991$).} On Track~2, DocVerify
achieves F1$_{\text{loc}} = 0.807 \pm 0.096$, comparable to the
challenge winner Sunlight ($0.784$); however, the margin ($+0.023$) is
well within the standard deviation of our estimates and should not be
interpreted as a definitive improvement. The high Track~2 variance is
driven by seeds where bonafide misclassifications generate false-positive
mask pixels; F1 on confirmed attack images is substantially more stable
($0.881 \pm 0.015$).
