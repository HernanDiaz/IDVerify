\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

All experiments are conducted on a single workstation running Windows~11,
equipped with an AMD Ryzen~5 3400G (3.70\,GHz), 32\,GB DDR4-3200 RAM, and an NVIDIA
RTX~5060\,Ti (16\,GB VRAM), using PyTorch 2.10.0 with CUDA 13.0 and
Optuna~3.x. Training uses a batch size of 64 and no data augmentation
\rev{(standard augmentation, rotation, brightness, contrast, blur and JPEG,
significantly degraded every metric on the FantasyID holdout;
see~\citep{docverify_zenodo})}.
All images are resized to $224\!\times\!224$ pixels and normalised to
$[0,1]$ prior to caching in GPU memory.
The full NCV pipeline required
approximately 59~hours; VRAM caching reduces per-trial training time from
$\sim$12 to $\sim$3.5 minutes. \rev{This 59-hour budget covers the full
scientific protocol; retraining the single deployed model from scratch takes
$7.7 \pm 1.0$~min.}

\subsection{Nested Cross-Validation Results}
\label{subsec:ncv_results}

Table~\ref{tab:nested_cv} reports per-fold test metrics. PR-AUC is
consistently high across all folds ($0.977$--$0.999$, mean $0.9921 \pm
0.0058$). Dice shows slightly more fold-to-fold variability ($0.800$--$0.909$,
mean $0.856 \pm 0.030$), reflecting the difficulty of pixel-level localization.
The Pareto-selected $\lambda_{\text{mask}}$ clusters between 2.3 and 2.7 in
eight of ten folds; the two outlier folds (F5, F9) correspond to the two lowest
Dice scores, confirming that $\lambda_{\text{mask}}$ is a decisive driver of
localization quality.

\subsection{Final Model Configuration}
\label{subsec:final_config}

\rev{The cross-validation above yields ten fold-specific configurations and
estimates generalization, but the deployed system is a single model. We select
the configuration with the smallest inner-CV distance to the ideal point
($1,1$) across all outer folds (Eq.~\ref{eq:ideal_point};
Table~\ref{tab:hpo_space}), which fixes both
the architecture (decoder width) and the Pareto-optimized loss weight
$\lambda_{\text{mask}}=2.46$, consistent with the $2.3$--$2.7$ cluster of
Section~\ref{subsec:ncv_results}. This final configuration is retrained from
scratch on the development set (up to 100 epochs with early stopping, as in
Section~\ref{subsec:ncv}) and evaluated on the held-out 15\% blind-test
set; we repeat the retraining over 30 independent seeds to quantify
run-to-run variability.}

\subsection{Ablation and Selection-Criterion Study}
\label{subsec:ablation}

To isolate each design decision, we evaluate this final model against five
variants under the same 30-seed blind-test protocol
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Three variants probe the architecture and loss
weighting: \texttt{cls\_only} (classification head only); \texttt{seg\_only}
(segmentation head only, detection by thresholding mean mask probability);
and \texttt{unweighted\_losses} (full multitask, $\lambda_{\text{cls}} =
\lambda_{\text{mask}} = 0.5$, bypassing HPO). \rev{Two further variants probe
the HPO selection criterion applied to the \emph{same} 500 MOTPE trials:
\texttt{by\_prauc} (the trial maximizing validation PR-AUC) and
\texttt{by\_dice} (the trial maximizing validation Dice), against our Pareto
criterion (distance to the ideal point).}

Regarding the architecture and loss variants, three findings stand out.
First, joint multitask training is essential:
\texttt{cls\_only} produces near-zero Dice ($0.002 \pm 0.008$). Second, a
dedicated classification head is necessary: \texttt{seg\_only} achieves
competitive Dice ($0.867$) but PR-AUC drops to $0.728 \pm 0.053$, confirming
that the segmentation logit alone is an unreliable detection signal. Third,
Pareto-optimized weights outperform equal weights specifically on localization:
\texttt{unweighted\_losses} matches the full model on PR-AUC ($p = 0.083$) but
shows significantly lower Dice ($p = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}$, $d = 1.01$, large
effect), validating the HPO contribution.

A key question for any multi-objective HPO framework is whether the added
complexity over simpler scalar alternatives yields measurable gains. \rev{Pareto
significantly outperforms both scalar criteria after Holm correction:
it improves localization over \texttt{by\_prauc} (Dice $+0.016$, $d=0.59$) and
improves \emph{both} objectives over \texttt{by\_dice} (PR-AUC $+0.0019$,
$d=0.64$; Dice $+0.018$, $d=1.00$, large effect).}

Beyond predictive accuracy, Pareto selection requires no a priori commitment to a
primary objective, making the full trade-off landscape observable
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}) and remaining structurally consistent with the DeepID
challenge protocol, which ranks detection and localization independently.

\subsection{Comparison with Related Systems}
\label{subsec:challenge}

\rev{DocVerify was developed after the DeepID 2025
Challenge~\citep{deepid2025challenge} deadline, so official test-set
evaluation was not possible; we report results on our internal 15\% holdout
(30 seeds), which differs from the official challenge set, making any comparison
indicative rather than controlled. Training data also differ markedly. The leading
entries rely on external data: Sunlight (1st) augmented FantasyID with over
60\,000 identity images, while the third-place EdgeDoc+TruFor
fusion~\citep{edgedoc2025} and the TruFor baseline~\citep{trufor2023} build on
a backbone pre-trained on $\sim$828\,000 manipulation images. EdgeDoc
(standalone), UAM-Biometrics, and DocVerify instead train on FantasyID alone,
the most directly comparable group. With these caveats, DocVerify reaches
detection F1@0.5 \rev{(the F1 score, $2PR/(P{+}R)$, of the bona-fide/attack
decision at classification threshold $0.5$)} $=0.969 \pm 0.014$ and localization
F1$_{\text{loc}}$ \rev{(pixel-level F1 of the predicted mask)}
$= 0.807 \pm 0.096$, in line with the challenge winner
Sunlight (F1@0.5 $0.991$, F1$_{\text{loc}}$ $0.784$) and ahead of the
FantasyID-only entries (EdgeDoc $0.43$, UAM-Biometrics $0.712$), despite using
no external data; we make no superiority claim given the differing evaluation
sets.}

\subsection{\rev{Generalization}}
\label{subsec:generalization}

\rev{Two experiments probe whether the approach generalizes beyond its specific
setting. \emph{Across architectures}, we re-ran the identical MOTPE protocol
(50 trials, 5 inner splits, Pareto selection, 30-seed blind test; a single study
rather than the full nested loop, to bound cost) with the encoder replaced by an
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 feeding the same U-Net decoder. MOTPE selected
$\lambda_{\text{mask}} = 2.69$ (vs.\ $2.46$ for DocVerify), so the Pareto balance
is consistent across backbones. It reaches PR-AUC $0.994 \pm 0.003$ and Dice
$0.889 \pm 0.011$ (30 seeds); paired Wilcoxon\,+\,Holm gives DocVerify a small
PR-AUC edge ($d = 0.75$) and ResNet-18 a Dice/F1 edge ($d = 0.60$--$0.67$), with
balanced accuracy tied. Neither dominates, so the selection methodology
generalizes across architectures~\citep{docverify_zenodo}. \emph{Across datasets},
we evaluate the FantasyID blind-test models zero-shot on the out-of-domain SIDTD
dataset~\citep{sidtd2024}, using only its \emph{templates} subset (2\,222 images:
1\,000 bonafide, 1\,222 attack) rather than the full SIDTD video collection.
Zero-shot performance drops to near chance (PR-AUC $0.555 \pm 0.011$, ROC-AUC
$0.504 \pm 0.009$): the FantasyID$\rightarrow$SIDTD domain gap is too wide for a
model trained on FantasyID alone. Retraining the same architecture on an 80/20
stratified split of the templates subset (1\,777 train, 445 test) recovers PR-AUC
$0.878 \pm 0.016$, ROC-AUC $0.826 \pm 0.023$, whereas a few-shot sweep
(10--400 images) stays at chance: only full in-domain training closes the gap, so
it reflects domain shift, not architectural capacity. The zero-shot scope of the
current FantasyID-trained system is thus bounded to its training domain.}
