Reviewer 2: Article: Multi-Objective Pareto Hyperparameter Optimization for Joint Detection and Localization of Forged Identity Documents

Summary: The author has substantially improved the manuscript. The most serious transparency concerns from the first round, i.e., the misattributed EdgeDoc figure, the selective omission of SIDTD results, and the omission of the TruFor fine-tuned baseline, have been addressed, including the disclosure of unfavorable results (near-chance zero-shot performance on SIDTD, PR-AUC = 0.555; performance degradation under standard augmentation). This is commendable and reflects good scientific practice rather than results-driven storytelling.

The methodological rigor (10×5 nested cross-validation, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction, Cohen's d, 30-seed blind evaluation, open code and archived per-seed CSVs) remains a strength and now extends to a second backbone (ResNet-18), supporting the claim that the multi-objective HPO methodology is architecture-agnostic.

However, one important issue remains only partially resolved. In Section 4.4, the author claims that "Pareto significantly outperforms both scalar criteria after Holm correction," yet the reported table shows that several comparisons against by_prauc (PR-AUC, balanced accuracy, F1-attack) remain non-significant even after correction, and one p-value (F1-macro = 0.050) sits exactly at the significance threshold. The blanket phrasing overstates what is, in fact, a partial and metric-specific advantage with mostly small-to-medium effect sizes (d ≈ 0.4-0.65), except for the Dice comparison against by_dice (d = 1.00). Given that this is the paper's central methodological claim, the author must recalibrate the language to precisely state which metrics were significant and which were not. Additionally, he should discuss the practical magnitude of the gains alongside the statistical significance.

Other points that deserve attention are the following:
1. The cross-domain augmentation check (Supplementary Table S8), which shows that augmentation does not close the domain gap on SIDTD, is highly relevant to the paper's generalization narrative and deserves at least a brief mention in the main text rather than only in the supplement.

2. Several quantitative justifications (computational cost breakdown, full pos_weight sensitivity table) currently exist only in the response letter, and should be incorporated, even summarized, into the supplementary material accompanying the final published version, to ensure long-term traceability independent of the review correspondence.

3. The manuscript's abstract should more clearly acknowledge the bounded, in-domain scope of the reported performance, given the near-chance zero-shot cross-domain result.

All other reviewer concerns, that is, dataset imbalance justification and sensitivity analysis, holdout protocol timing, architecture description completeness, qualitative failure-case analysis, citation accuracy, and the removal of the non-comparable challenge benchmark table, have been adequately and convincingly addressed.

Recommendation: Minor revision.

The vast majority of prior concerns have been resolved with genuine empirical evidence, including honestly reported negative results.

However, the author should perform the following adequations:
1. Rephrasing the claims in Section 4.4 regarding Pareto vs. scalar selection to accurately reflect the mixed statistical significance and moderate effect sizes rather than an unqualified "significant advantage".

2. Briefly integrating the cross-domain augmentation result (Table S8) into the main discussion of generalization.

3. Ensuring that quantitative justifications currently confined to the response letter are also available in the supplementary material of the published version.

These are editorial and interpretive refinements rather than new experiments, and do not warrant a further major revision cycle.
