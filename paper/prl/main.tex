\title{Multi-Objective Pareto Hyperparameter Optimization for Joint Detection and
       Localization of Forged Identity Documents}

\author[1]{Hern\'{a}n D\'{i}az Rodr\'{i}guez\corref{cor1}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\ead{diazhernan@uniovi.es}

\address[1]{Department of Computer Science, University of Oviedo, Asturias, Spain}

\begin{abstract}
Automated forensic analysis of identity documents requires simultaneously
detecting whether a document has been tampered with and identifying which
regions were altered. These two objectives are structurally coupled but exist
in tension: optimizing one with a fixed loss weight may degrade the other.
We propose DocVerify, a multi-task convolutional network paired with a
multi-objective hyperparameter optimization (HPO) framework that treats
detection quality (PR-AUC) and localization quality (Dice) as simultaneous
objectives. Using the Multiobjective Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (MOTPE)
within a 10-fold nested cross-validation protocol (50 trials per outer fold,
500 unique configurations evaluated across 5 inner splits — 2\,500 individual
model trainings in total), DocVerify selects configurations from the
Pareto-optimal front by minimizing Euclidean distance to the ideal point
$(1,1)$, without any manual weighting. Evaluated on the FantasyID benchmark
\citep{fantasyid2024}, DocVerify achieves PR-AUC $= 0.9921 \pm 0.0058$ and
Dice $= 0.856 \pm 0.030$ under nested cross-validation, and detection
F1@0.5 $= 0.969 \pm 0.014$ with localization F1 $= 0.807 \pm 0.096$ on the
blind test set (30 seeds). An ablation confirms that Pareto-optimized loss
weighting provides a statistically significant Dice improvement over
equal-weight joint training ($p = 1.3 \times 10^{-4}$, $d = 1.01$, large
effect) while maintaining equivalent detection accuracy.
\end{abstract}
