FIGURE 3 — Ablation study caption (for the paper)
==================================================

Suggested caption:
------------------
Ablation study comparing four model variants across 30 independent random seeds
on the blind test set. Each violin shows the full distribution of (left) PR-AUC
and (right) global Dice scores; individual seed results are overlaid as jittered
dots. Significance symbols above each violin indicate the result of a two-sided
Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Holm correction against the Multitask baseline
(*** p < 0.001; ns p >= 0.05). The Multitask variant (Pareto-weighted joint
training) achieves the highest mean performance on both metrics and is
significantly superior to all other variants except Unweighted on PR-AUC
(p = 0.083).


What the figure shows (for the Methods / Ablation section):
------------------------------------------------------------
The ablation study isolates the contribution of each design choice in the
DocVerify architecture by evaluating four variants under identical conditions
(same dataset splits, same 30 random seeds, same evaluation protocol):

  - Multitask: the full proposed model, jointly trained on classification and
    segmentation with loss weights selected by Pareto-based HPO. This is the
    reference variant.

  - cls_only: the classification head is retained but the segmentation head and
    its loss are removed entirely. This variant can detect forgeries (producing
    a scalar anomaly score) but cannot localise them (no pixel-level mask).

  - seg_only: the segmentation head is retained but the classification head and
    its loss are removed. Detection is performed by thresholding the aggregated
    mask probability rather than a dedicated classification logit.

  - unweighted_losses: the full multitask architecture is kept, but the
    classification and segmentation losses are combined with equal weights
    (lambda = 0.5 each) instead of the Pareto-optimised weights. This isolates
    the contribution of the HPO weighting scheme from the multitask architecture
    itself.

Statistical testing uses the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-parametric,
appropriate for bounded metrics) with Holm-Bonferroni correction for multiple
comparisons (three tests per metric). Effect sizes are reported as mean +/- std
over 30 seeds.


Key observations for the Results section:
------------------------------------------
- cls_only collapses to near-zero Dice (mean 0.0018 +/- 0.008), confirming that
  the segmentation head is essential for localisation. Its PR-AUC (0.950 +/-
  0.074) is significantly lower than Multitask (p < 0.001) and shows high
  variance, suggesting that removing the regularising effect of the joint
  segmentation loss destabilises detection training.

- seg_only achieves competitive Dice (0.867 +/- 0.023, p = 0.288 vs Multitask)
  but its PR-AUC drops substantially (0.728 +/- 0.053, p < 0.001), demonstrating
  that a dedicated classification head is necessary for reliable detection, and
  that the segmentation logit alone is an insufficient detection signal.

- unweighted_losses matches Multitask on PR-AUC (0.996 vs 0.997, p = 0.083,
  not significant) but shows significantly lower Dice (0.857 vs 0.875,
  p < 0.001), indicating that Pareto-based loss weighting provides a measurable
  benefit specifically for localisation quality without compromising detection.

- Taken together, the ablation confirms that all three design choices contribute:
  (1) the joint multitask objective, (2) the dedicated classification head, and
  (3) the Pareto-optimised loss weights. The Multitask variant with Pareto
  weighting is the only configuration that achieves strong performance on both
  the detection and localisation objectives simultaneously.
