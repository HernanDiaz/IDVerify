FIGURE 5 — Pareto vs. scalar HPO caption (for the paper)
=========================================================

Suggested caption:
------------------
Comparison of multi-objective Pareto HPO against scalar grid search across 10
outer folds of the nested cross-validation. Box plots show the distribution of
(left) PR-AUC and (right) global Dice; individual fold results are overlaid as
jittered dots. Scalar variants select the configuration that maximises a single
objective: either PR-AUC ("Scalar best PR-AUC") or Dice ("Scalar best Dice").
Significance symbols above each scalar box indicate Wilcoxon signed-rank tests
with Holm correction against the Pareto baseline (ns: p >= 0.05, n = 10 folds).
No statistically significant differences were found between methods.


What the figure shows (for the Methods / Results section):
----------------------------------------------------------
The figure evaluates whether the multi-objective Pareto selection criterion
confers a measurable advantage over simpler scalar alternatives on this dataset.

Three HPO selection strategies are compared, all using the same 50 MOTPE trials
per inner fold and the same nested CV outer splits:

  - Pareto (ours): configuration selected as the Pareto-optimal trial minimising
    Euclidean distance to the ideal point (1, 1) in the (PR-AUC, Dice) space.
    This criterion does not commit to a single objective a priori.

  - Scalar (best PR-AUC): from all 50 trials per fold, the configuration with
    the highest inner-validation PR-AUC is selected, ignoring Dice entirely.

  - Scalar (best Dice): from all 50 trials per fold, the configuration with the
    highest inner-validation Dice is selected, ignoring PR-AUC entirely.

Statistical testing uses the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test (non-parametric,
paired across folds) with Holm-Bonferroni correction for two comparisons per
metric. With n = 10 paired observations (outer folds), the test is low-powered;
effect sizes below approximately 0.01 cannot be reliably detected.


Key observations and honest interpretation for the Results / Discussion section:
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Numerically, all three methods achieve closely matched performance:
    Pareto:            PR-AUC 0.9921 +/- 0.0060,  Dice 0.8555 +/- 0.0345
    Scalar by_prauc:   PR-AUC 0.9958 +/- 0.0015,  Dice 0.8574 +/- 0.0166
    Scalar by_dice:    PR-AUC 0.9936 +/- 0.0033,  Dice 0.8630 +/- 0.0147

- Pareto shows slightly lower means on both metrics (delta PR-AUC approx -0.004,
  delta Dice approx -0.002 to -0.008 vs scalar variants), but none of these
  differences reach statistical significance (all p >= 0.05 after Holm
  correction).

- Pareto also exhibits higher variance than scalar methods (std 0.006 vs 0.002
  on PR-AUC; 0.035 vs 0.015-0.017 on Dice), which may reflect sensitivity to
  the random composition of inner folds with only 10 outer splits.

IMPORTANT — honest framing for the paper:
  The Pareto approach does NOT outperform scalar selection in absolute metric
  terms on this dataset. The contribution of Pareto HPO should therefore be
  framed around methodological advantages rather than performance gains:

  (1) Objective-agnostic selection: no single metric is privileged before
      training. The criterion is consistent with the DeepID 2025 evaluation
      protocol, which ranks systems independently on Track 1 and Track 2.

  (2) Transparency: the selection is justified by position in the full
      objective space rather than by an arbitrary scalar weight lambda.

  (3) Richer diagnostics: the Pareto front (Figure 1) reveals the full
      trade-off landscape across 500 trials, enabling post-hoc analysis of
      alternative solutions — something scalar search cannot provide.

  Suggested phrasing for the Discussion:
  "Pareto HPO achieves performance statistically indistinguishable from scalar
  selection on both metrics (all p >= 0.05, Wilcoxon + Holm, n = 10), with
  absolute differences below 0.01 on PR-AUC and Dice. The advantage of the
  multi-objective approach lies not in raw performance but in the principled,
  objective-agnostic nature of the selection criterion and the richer
  characterisation of the HPO landscape it provides."
